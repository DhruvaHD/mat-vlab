Impact Test (Charpy & Izod)

Determine the notch toughness, dynamic shock resistance, and temperature-dependent Ductile-to-Brittle Transition Temperature (DBTT) of metallic materials by measuring kinetic energy absorbed ($K_V$) during high-velocity pendulum fracture of standardized notched specimens.

01

Experiment Objective

1. To measure the energy absorbed ($K_V$) and calculate the notch toughness ($a_k$) of standard metallic specimens (Mild Steel, Medium Carbon Steel, Alloy Steel 4140, Stainless Steel 304, Aluminium 6061-T6, and Grey Cast Iron) under dynamic three-point bending impact according to ASTM E23 / ISO 148-1.

2. To evaluate the effect of temperature on fracture behavior, identify the Ductile-to-Brittle Transition Temperature (DBTT) in Body-Centered Cubic (BCC) alloys, calculate Fracture Appearance Transition Temperature ($FATT_{50}$), and correlate lateral expansion with metallurgical toughness criteria.

02

Theory & Fundamental Concepts

Charpy vs. Izod Impact Testing
  • Charpy Test (ASTM E23 / ISO 148): The specimen ($10 \times 10 \times 55\text{ mm}$) rests horizontally as a three-point simply supported beam on 40 mm span anvils. The striking hammer strikes directly behind the V-notch. Standard in structural steel, pressure vessels, and aerospace.
  • Izod Test (ASTM E23 / ISO 180): The specimen ($10 \times 10 \times 75\text{ mm}$) is clamped vertically as a cantilever beam with the notch facing the striking direction. Widely used for plastics, tool steels, and foundry castings.
The DBTT Phenomenon & Crystal Structure
  • BCC Metals (Ferritic Steels): Dislocation mobility has high thermal activation resistance (Peierls-Nabarro stress). At low temperatures, yield stress exceeds cleavage stress, causing sudden drop from high energy (Upper Shelf) to catastrophic brittle fracture (Lower Shelf).
  • FCC Metals (Aluminium, SS 304): Possess 12 independent slip systems active across all temperatures. They do not exhibit DBTT drop, remaining ductile and tough down to cryogenic conditions (-196°C).

Governing Mathematical Principles

1. Absorbed Energy ($K_V$)
\(K_V = E_0 - E_1 - L_f\)

Where $E_0$ is initial potential energy ($m \cdot g \cdot h_0$), $E_1$ is residual swing energy ($m \cdot g \cdot h_1$), and $L_f$ is tare friction & windage loss.

2. Specific Notch Toughness ($a_k$)
\(a_k = \frac{K_V}{A_0}\)

Energy absorbed divided by original net cross-sectional area under the notch ($A_0 = 0.80\text{ cm}^2$). Expressed in $\text{J/cm}^2$ or $\text{kJ/m}^2$.

3. DBTT Sigmoidal Model
\(K_V(T) = A + B \tanh\left(\frac{T - T_0}{C}\right)\)

Hyperbolic tangent function fitting the sigmoidal transition between lower shelf and upper shelf toughness across temperatures.

06

Standard Operating Procedure (ASTM E23 SOP)

1
Friction Tare Calibration:
Cock pendulum to release angle ($140^\circ$) without specimen, release freely, and verify tare pointer reads $\le 1.5\text{ J}$ friction loss.
2
Specimen Temperature Conditioning:
Condition specimen in liquid nitrogen/ethanol bath or temperature chamber for minimum 15 minutes to ensure uniform thermal stabilization.
3
Rapid Anvil Alignment (<5 Seconds):
Transfer specimen with centering tongs onto the 40 mm span anvils. Ensure notch faces away from the pendulum striking edge.
4
Pendulum Release & Braking:
Close safety cage, disengage release latch, fracture the specimen, apply mechanical brake on return swing, and record $K_V$ on pointer.
