Experiment Objective

1. To determine the compressive stress-strain curve, compressive modulus of elasticity ($E_c$), and 0.2% offset compressive yield strength ($\sigma_{cy}$) for ductile metallic alloys (Mild Steel, Aluminium 6061-T6, Brass, Copper) and the Ultimate Compressive Strength ($\sigma_{cu}$) for brittle metals (Grey Cast Iron) under controlled axial loading according to ASTM E9.

2. To observe and quantify interfacial frictional constraint effects (specimen barreling index $B$), evaluate slenderness ratio ($h_0 / d_0$) constraints to prevent column buckling, and analyze brittle shear failure modes along ~45° maximum shear planes (Coulomb-Mohr fracture criterion).

02

Theory & Constitutive Mechanics

Platen Friction & Specimen Barreling

During uniaxial compression, as the specimen shortens axially, Poisson's effect dictates radial expansion. However, interfacial friction between the hardened loading platens and specimen end-faces creates a transverse shearing restraint. This generates localized triaxial compressive stress cones at the ends, leaving the unconstrained mid-height region to bulge outwards freely. This geometry is known as barreling ($B = (d_{mid} - d_{end}) / d_0$).

Ductile Flow vs. Brittle 45° Shear Fracture

Ductile metals (Mild Steel, Copper): Exhibit continuous plastic strain-hardening without discrete necking or fracture, flattening into pancakes as apparent flow stress climbs.
Brittle metals (Grey Cast Iron): Because graphite flakes are pressed together in compression rather than opened as crack-tips, compressive strength (~680 MPa) is 3 to 4 times higher than tensile strength (~200 MPa). Failure occurs catastrophically by shear sliding along ~45° planes (Coulomb-Mohr criterion).

Governing Mathematical Equations

1. Compressive Stress ($\sigma_c$)
\(\sigma_c = \frac{F}{A_0}\)

Axial force $F$ divided by original cross-sectional area $A_0 = \pi d_0^2 / 4$. Expressed in MPa ($\text{N/mm}^2$).

2. Compressive Strain ($\varepsilon_c$)
\(\varepsilon_c = \frac{\Delta h}{h_0} = \frac{h_0 - h}{h_0}\)

Reduction in specimen height divided by original height. Dimensionless or expressed in percentage (%).

3. Compressive Modulus ($E_c$)
\(E_c = \frac{\Delta \sigma_c}{\Delta \varepsilon_c}\)

Linear slope of initial elastic regime determined via least-squares linear regression (GPa).

4. Barreling Index ($B$)
\(B = \frac{d_{mid} - d_{end}}{d_0}\)

Measures severity of interfacial friction between platens and specimen ends.

06

Standard Operating Procedure (ASTM E9 SOP)

1
Specimen Dimension Metrology:
Measure $d_0$ at three heights with an optical micrometer; verify end-face flatness and parallelism ($<0.005\text{ mm}$).
2
Platen Lubrication & Centering:
Apply thin PTFE film or molybdenum grease to platens. Position specimen strictly concentric with lower platen centering rings.
3
Spherical Seat Self-Alignment:
Apply minor seating preload (50–100 N) to allow the spherical seat to swivel into 100% planar contact, then zero LVDT deflectometer.
4
Constant Strain-Rate Loading:
Drive crosshead at $0.005\text{ mm/mm/min}$ in elastic range until fracture (cast iron) or prescribed plastic strain (steel/aluminium).
