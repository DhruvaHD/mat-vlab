Experiment Objective

To determine the Rockwell Hardness (HR) of various ferrous and non-ferrous alloys using the differential-depth penetration method on standardized Rockwell scales: Scale B (HRB) for softer engineering alloys (annealed mild steel, brass, aluminium) and Scale C (HRC) for hardened tool steels, verify specimen thickness limits ($t \ge 10e$), and interpret direct indicator readings.

02

Theory & Principle of Rockwell Testing

The Rockwell hardness test (developed by Hugh and Stanley Rockwell in 1919) determines hardness by measuring the permanent net depth of indentation ($e$) produced by an indenter under a major load relative to a pre-applied minor load. Because depth measurement eliminates optical diameter measuring steps, the test takes under 10 seconds and leaves a tiny, virtually non-destructive indentation.

The standard test cycle proceeds through three distinct stages:

  1. Preliminary Minor Load ($F_0 = 10\text{ kgf}$): Seats the indenter through surface oxide, roughness, and mechanical play to establish a zero depth datum ($h_0$). The dial indicator is zeroed (SET point).
  2. Total Major Load ($F = F_0 + F_1$): Additional load ($F_1$) is applied smoothly via a hydraulic dashpot (e.g. 90 kgf for Scale B, 140 kgf for Scale C). The indenter penetrates to maximum depth ($h_1$).
  3. Unloading to Minor Load ($F_0$): The major load is released while the minor load is maintained. Elastic deformation recovers, leaving permanent plastic depth ($h_2$). The net permanent increase is $e = h_2 - h_0$.
$$HR = N - \frac{e}{0.002\text{ mm}}$$
Where 1 dial unit = 0.002 mm (2 µm); $N = 100$ for Scale C and A; $N = 130$ for Scale B.

Standard Rockwell Scales (ASTM E18)

The Rockwell test provides multiple scales designated by letters. Each scale represents a unique pairing of indenter type, minor load, major load, and dial scale:

Standard Operating Procedure (ASTM E18)

Step 1: Mount Specimen
Clean Seating

Ensure specimen is flat, clean, and deburred. Mount firmly on the anvil directly perpendicular to the indenter axis.

Step 2: Minor Load (10 kgf)
Zeroing to SET

Turn handwheel clockwise to raise the specimen until the dial indicator small pointer reaches the dot and large pointer aligns with SET (0).

Step 3: Apply Major Load
Dashpot Tripping

Trip the operating lever to release the major load (e.g. 100 kgf or 150 kgf). The hydraulic dashpot smoothly regulates descent without impact. Dwell for 2–6s.

Step 4: Unload & Read
Direct Readout

Pull the operating lever back to release the major load (retaining minor load). Read the indicated hardness number directly on the appropriate scale.

Step-by-Step Mathematical Calculation

Example: Hardened Medium Carbon Steel on Rockwell Scale C (150 kgf total load):
1. Recorded Dial Reading: $HRC = 48.5$
2. Net permanent indentation depth: $e = (N - HRC) \cdot 0.002\text{ mm} = (100 - 48.5) \cdot 0.002 = 51.5 \cdot 0.002 = \mathbf{0.1030\text{ mm}}\ (103\ \mu\text{m})$
3. Minimum Specimen Thickness: $t_{min} = 10 \cdot e = 10 \cdot 0.1030 = \mathbf{1.03\text{ mm}}$
4. Standard Designation: $\mathbf{48.5\text{ HRC}}$
