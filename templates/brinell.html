Brinell Hardness Test (HBW)

Determine the surface indentation hardness of metallic materials by forcing a hardened tungsten carbide ball into the test specimen under a heavy static normal load ($P$), and measuring the resulting spherical impression diameter ($d$) using an optical measuring microscope.

Experiment Objective

To determine the Brinell Hardness Number (HBW) of various ferrous and non-ferrous engineering alloys (Mild Steel, Medium Carbon Steel, Grey Cast Iron, Aluminium 6061-T6, Cartridge Brass, and Copper) using a standard tungsten carbide spherical indenter under specified normal loads, verify indentation geometric validity ($0.24 \le d/D \le 0.60$), and correlate measured hardness with empirical tensile strength ($\sigma_{uts} \approx 3.45 \cdot HBW$).

02

Theory & Principle of Brinell Testing

In the Brinell hardness test (invented by Swedish engineer Johan August Brinell in 1900), a precision ground, hardened spherical ball indenter of diameter $D$ is pressed into the flat, polished surface of a test metal by an applied normal load $P$. The load is maintained for a designated dwell period (typically 10 to 15 seconds for ferrous metals and 30 seconds for soft non-ferrous metals).

After the load is released, a spherical cap indentation remains. The curved contact surface area ($A$) of this impression is:

$$HBW = \frac{2P}{\pi D \left(D - \sqrt{D^2 - d^2}\right)}$$
Where:
  • $P$ = Applied normal test force in kilogram-force ($\text{kgf}$) [or $1\text{ kgf} = 9.80665\text{ N}$]
  • $D$ = Diameter of the tungsten carbide indenter ball in millimeters ($\text{mm}$) [Standard: 10 mm, 5 mm, 2.5 mm]
  • $d$ = Arithmetic mean diameter of the impression in millimeters: $d = \frac{d_1 + d_2}{2}$
  • $HBW$ = Hardness Brinell Wolfram-Carbide (ASTM E10 designation)

Specimen Requirements & Testing Limitations

Thickness ($t \ge 8h$)

Specimen thickness must be at least 8 to 10 times the indentation depth $h = (D - \sqrt{D^2 - d^2})/2$ to prevent the "anvil effect" (where the support anvil distorts the bottom face).

Edge Distance ($\ge 2.5d$)

The distance from the center of any indentation to the edge of the specimen must be at least 2.5 times the indentation diameter ($d$) to prevent unconstrained plastic edge bulging.

Indent Spacing ($\ge 3d$)

The distance between centers of adjacent indentations must be at least 3.0 times $d$ to avoid measuring work-hardened plastic deformation zones from prior tests.

Upper Limit ($≤ 650$ HBW)

Brinell testing is limited to materials with hardness under 650 HBW. Testing harder metals causes flattening and elastic spalling of the tungsten carbide ball indenter.

05

Standard Operating Procedure (ASTM E10)

Step 1: Surface Preparation
Grinding & Mounting

Grind and polish the specimen face with emery paper (up to 400 grit) to remove mill scale, oxide films, and decarburized layers. Ensure top and bottom surfaces are parallel. Place sample flat on the anvil.

Step 2: Elevation & Loading
Load Dwell Cycle

Turn the handwheel clockwise to raise the anvil until the specimen contacts the ball indenter. Actuate the hydraulic/deadweight loading lever. Maintain the designated load (e.g. 3000 kgf) for 10–15 seconds dwell.

Step 3: Optical Measurement
Reticle Reading & HBW

Release the load and lower the specimen. Position the 20x optical measuring microscope over the impression. Align reticle crosshairs to measure two perpendicular diameters $d_1$ and $d_2$. Calculate mean $d$ and compute HBW.

Step-by-Step Mathematical Calculation

Example Evaluation for Mild Steel under 3000 kgf load ($D = 10\text{ mm}$, $d = 5.18\text{ mm}$):
1. Calculate term: $\sqrt{D^2 - d^2} = \sqrt{10.0^2 - 5.18^2} = \sqrt{100 - 26.8324} = \sqrt{73.1676} = 8.5538\text{ mm}$
2. Denominator: $\pi \cdot D \cdot (D - \sqrt{D^2 - d^2}) = 3.14159 \cdot 10.0 \cdot (10.0 - 8.5538) = 31.4159 \cdot 1.4462 = 45.4338\text{ mm}^2$
3. Brinell Hardness: $HBW = \frac{2 \cdot 3000}{45.4338} = \frac{6000}{45.4338} = \mathbf{132.1\text{ HBW}}$
4. Indentation Depth: $h = \frac{D - \sqrt{D^2 - d^2}}{2} = \frac{10.0 - 8.5538}{2} = \mathbf{0.7231\text{ mm}}$
5. Minimum Specimen Thickness: $t_{min} = 8 \cdot h = 8 \cdot 0.7231 = \mathbf{5.78\text{ mm}}$
6. Tensile Strength Estimation: $\sigma_{uts} \approx 3.45 \cdot 132.1 = \mathbf{455.7\text{ MPa}}$
