Spherical Ball Indenter ASTM E10 / ISO 6506

1. Brinell Hardness Test

Applies a static normal load (up to 3000 kgf) through a 10 mm tungsten carbide ball (HBW) into the specimen surface. The arithmetic mean of two perpendicular indentation diameters ($d_1, d_2$) is measured using an optical measuring microscope to compute surface contact pressure.

Indenter: 10 mm, 5 mm, 2.5 mm WC Ball
Standard Load: 500 to 3000 kgf (P/D² = 30, 10, 5)
Measurement: Optical Microscope (d₁, d₂)
Best Suited: Cast Iron, Steels, Forgings

Differential Depth Measurement ASTM E18 / ISO 6508

2. Rockwell Hardness Test

Measures the net permanent increase in penetration depth ($e$) between a preliminary minor load ($F_0 = 10\text{ kgf}$) and a total major load ($F_0 + F_1 = 60, 100, 150\text{ kgf}$). Eliminates optical operator bias and provides direct dial or digital indicator readout in seconds.

Indenters: 120° Diamond Cone / 1/16" Ball
Primary Scales: Scale B (HRB) & Scale C (HRC)
Measurement: Direct Depth Dial (1 div = 0.002 mm)
Best Suited: Hardened Steels, Brass, Finished Parts

Comparison Feature Brinell Hardness Test (HBW) Rockwell Hardness Test (HR)
Standard Specification ASTM E10, ISO 6506-1, IS 1500 ASTM E18, ISO 6508-1, IS 1586
Indenter Geometry Tungsten Carbide (WC) Ball: 10 mm, 5 mm, or 2.5 mm diameter Diamond Brale Cone (120° apex, 0.2 mm radius) or 1/16" (1.588 mm) WC Ball
Loading Cycle Single static major load (typically 3000, 1500, or 500 kgf) held for 10–15s dwell Two-stage cycle: Preliminary minor load (10 kgf) + Total major load (60, 100, 150 kgf)
Measurement Principle Measures optical diameter (d) of spherical cap contact area ($A = \pi D h$) Measures net permanent depth increase (e) after releasing major load
Hardness Equation HBW = 2P / [ πD (D - √(D² - d²)) ] HR = N - (e / 0.002 mm)
Indentation Impression Large spherical impression (2.5 to 6.0 mm dia); averages structural heterogeneity Very small mark (depth 0.05 to 0.2 mm); non-destructive to most finished parts
Primary Advantages Excellent for cast irons, coarse grain forgings; strong empirical correlation to UTS ($\sigma_{uts} \approx 3.45 \cdot HBW$) Fast test cycle (< 10s); direct digital/dial readout; no subjective optical reading errors; wide hardness range
Limitations Requires optical micrometer; leaves visible blemish; not suitable for very hard metals (> 650 HBW) Sensitive to surface roughness and indenter tip damage; not representative for coarse heterogeneous alloys
